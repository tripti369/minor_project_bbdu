# Employee Attrition — Business Notes

Dataset: employee_hr_cleaned.csv (2,764 employees)

Overall attrition (LeaveOrNot = 1) is 39.4% of the workforce.

Attrition by city is the standout signal: Pune is at 50.9% attrition,
noticeably higher than Bangalore (35.0%) and New Delhi (34.1%). Any
retention budget should be weighted toward the Pune office first.

Attrition by education level is counter-intuitive: PHD holders have the
*lowest* attrition at 25.6%, Bachelors sit at 37.5%, and Masters holders
have the *highest* attrition at 48.5%. This suggests Masters-level
employees may be the group most likely to be recruited away or to seek
career moves, and is worth a focused stay-interview program.

Employees who have ever been "benched" (without a project) leave more
often — 44.0% attrition versus 38.7% for employees who have never been
benched. Minimizing bench time, or giving benched employees meaningful
interim work, is a concrete lever to reduce attrition.

## Risk framing

Attrition above 35% combined with a below-90% forecast confidence should
be treated as a "Medium" or "High" risk signal in the executive decision
engine — it means both revenue predictability and workforce stability are
uncertain at the same time, which compounds planning risk for the next
two quarters.

## Recommended actions

- Prioritise a Pune-specific retention review given the ~15-point gap
  versus the other two cities.
- Build a stay-interview / career-pathing program aimed at Masters-level
  employees specifically.
- Reduce bench time or provide upskilling assignments during bench
  periods to close the 5-point attrition gap linked to being benched.
